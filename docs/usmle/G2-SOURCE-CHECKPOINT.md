## Key facts per topic (from text layer)
- **p79 modes**: AD = often structural genes; many generations, both sexes; pleiotropic + variably expressive; family history crucial; 1 affected heterozygous parent → 50%/child. AR = often enzyme deficiencies; usually 1 generation; more severe, childhood; ↑risk consanguineous; unaffected sib of affected = 2/3 carrier; 2 carrier parents → 25% affected / 50% carrier / 25% neither. XLR = sons of heterozygous mothers 50% affected; no male-to-male; skips generations; more severe in males; females usually must be homozygous. XLD = both parents transmit; affected mother → 50% each child; affected father → 100% daughters, 0% sons; examples fragile X (atypical), Alport, hypophosphatemic rickets (X-linked hypophosphatemia) — phosphate wasting at proximal tubule → ricketslike presentation. Mitochondrial = only through mother; all offspring of affected females may show signs; variable expression in population or family due to heteroplasmy; mtDNA mutations; examples mitochondrial myopathies, Leber.
- **p80 lists**: AD diseases: achondroplasia, ADPKD, familial adenomatous polyposis, familial hypercholesterolemia, hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (Osler-Weber-Rendu), hereditary spherocytosis, Huntington, Li-Fraumeni, Marfan, MEN, myotonic muscular dystrophy, NF1 (von Recklinghausen), NF2, tuberous sclerosis, von Hippel-Lindau. AR diseases: mostly enzyme defects — oculocutaneous albinism, PKU, CF, sickle cell, Wilson, sphingolipidoses (except Fabry), hemochromatosis, glycogen storage diseases, thalassemia, mucopolysaccharidoses (except Hunter), Friedreich ataxia, Kartagener, ARPKD.
- **p80 CF**: AR; CFTR gene chromosome 7; ΔF508 deletion; most common lethal genetic disease in patients with European ancestry. CFTR = ATP-gated Cl⁻ channel (secretes Cl⁻ in lungs/GI tract, reabsorbs Cl⁻ in sweat glands). Phe508 deletion → misfolded protein → improper protein trafficking → protein absent from cell membrane → ↓Cl⁻ (and H₂O and HCO₃⁻) secretion → compensatory ↑Na⁺ reabsorption via epithelial Na⁺ channels (ENaC) → ↑H₂O reabsorption → abnormally thick mucus into lungs/GI tract. ↑Na⁺ reabsorption → more negative transepithelial potential difference. Dx: ↑Cl⁻ concentration in pilocarpine-induced sweat test; contraction alkalosis + hypokalemia (ECF effects analogous to loop diuretic — ECF H₂O/Na⁺ losses via sweating + renal K⁺/H⁺ wasting); ↑immunoreactive trypsinogen (newborn screen, clogging of pancreatic duct). Complications: recurrent pulmonary infections (S aureus infancy/early childhood, P aeruginosa adulthood, ABPA), chronic bronchitis + bronchiectasis → reticulonodular CXR, sinus opacification, nasal polyps, nail clubbing; pancreatic insufficiency, malabsorption + steatorrhea, fat-soluble vitamin (A D E K) deficiencies → CF-related diabetes, biliary cirrhosis, liver disease; meconium ileus newborns; infertility males (absence of vas deferens, spermatogenesis may be unaffected), subfertility females (amenorrhea, thick cervical mucus). Tx: chest physiotherapy, aerosolized dornase alfa (DNase), inhaled hypertonic saline → mucus clearance; azithromycin prevents acute exacerbations; ibuprofen anti-inflammatory; pancreatic enzyme replacement (pancrelipase); CFTR modulators alone or in combination, efficacy varies by mutation (pharmacogenomics) — potentiators (hold gate of CFTR channel open → Cl⁻ flows through membrane; eg, ivaCaFToR) or correctors (help CFTR form right 3-D shape → moves to cell surface; eg, lumaCaFToR, tezaCaFToR).
- **p81 XLR list**: Bruton agammaglobulinemia, Duchenne AND Becker muscular dystrophies, Fabry, G6PD deficiency, hemophilia A and B, Hunter syndrome, Lesch-Nyhan, ocular albinism, OTC deficiency, Wiskott-Aldrich. Turner 45,XO females more likely to have XLR disorder. Lyonization: one X in each XX cell randomly deactivated → condensed into Barr body (methylated heterochromatin); skewed inactivation → XX individuals may express XLR diseases (eg, G6PD); penetrance/severity of XLD in XX may be impacted.
- **p81 Duchenne**: XLR typically frameshift deletions or nonsense mutations → truncated or absent dystrophin → progressive myofiber damage; can also result from splicing errors. Weakness begins in pelvic girdle muscles, progresses superiorly. Pseudohypertrophy of calves due to fibrofatty replacement of muscle. Waddling gait. Onset <5y. Dilated cardiomyopathy common cause of death. Gowers sign = uses upper extremities to help stand up; classically Duchenne but also other muscular dystrophies + inflammatory myopathies (eg, polymyositis). "Duchenne = deleted dystrophin." DMD = largest protein-coding human gene → ↑chance of spontaneous mutation. Dystrophin anchors muscle fibers to extracellular matrix, primarily skeletal + cardiac. Loss → myonecrosis. ↑CK and aldolase; genetic testing confirms. Becker: XLR typically NON-frameshift deletions (partially functional instead of truncated); less severe (Becker is Better); onset adolescence/early adulthood. Deletions can cause both; 2/3 of cases have large deletions spanning ≥1 exon. Myotonic dystrophy: AD; onset 20-30y; CTG repeat expansion in DMPK gene → abnormal expression of myotonin protein kinase → percussion myotonia (difficulty releasing hand from handshake), muscle wasting, cataracts, testicular atrophy, frontal balding, arrhythmia. Mnemonic: Cataracts, Toupee (early balding in males), Gonadal atrophy. Biopsy: ring fibers and central nuclei.
- **p82 mitochondrial diseases**: rare, 2° to failure in oxidative phosphorylation; ↑energy tissues preferentially affected (CNS, skeletal muscle). Mitochondrial myopathies incl. MELAS (mitochondrial encephalomyopathy with lactic acidosis and strokelike episodes), MERRF (myoclonic epilepsy with ragged red fibers). LM with stain: ragged red fibers due to compensatory proliferation of mitochondria. EM: mitochondrial crystalline inclusions. Leber hereditary optic neuropathy: mutations in complex I of ETC → neuronal death in retina + optic nerve → subacute bilateral vision loss in teens/young adults (males > females); usually permanent; may be accompanied by neurologic dysfunction (tremors, MS-like illness).
- **p82 Rett**: sporadic, de novo MECP2 on X; seen mostly in females; embryonically lethal in males; initial normal development (6-18 months) → regression ("retturn") in motor, verbal, cognitive abilities; ataxia; seizures; scoliosis; stereotypic hand-wringing.
- **p82 Fragile X**: X-linked (atypical) inheritance; trinucleotide repeats in FMR1 → hypermethylation of cytosine residues → ↓expression. Most common INHERITED cause of intellectual disability (Down syndrome most common genetic cause overall but mostly sporadic). Expansion (CGG)n occurs during oogenesis — "Fragile EGG (think CGG and oogenesis)". Premutation (50-200 repeats) → tremor, ataxia, 1° ovarian insufficiency. Full mutation (>200) → postpubertal macroorchidism (enlarged testes), long face with large jaw, large everted ears, autism, mitral valve prolapse, hypermobile joints. Self-mutilation common, can be confused with Lesch-Nyhan. Table: Huntington (CAG)n AD "Caudate has ↓ACh and GABA"; Myotonic dystrophy (CTG)n AD "Cataracts, Toupee, Gonadal atrophy in males, reduced fertility in females"; Fragile X (CGG)n XD "Chin (protruding), Giant Gonads"; Friedreich ataxia (GAA)n AR "Ataxic GAAit". Anticipation: disease severity ↑ and age of onset ↓ in successive generations.
