**1. Escherichia coli Intro Paragraph**
*   **Header:** *Escherichia coli* (Bold, Italic, Left-aligned)
*   **Text:** "Gram ⊖, indole ⊕ rod. *E coli* virulence factors: fimbriae (ie, **P** pili)—cystitis and **p**yelonephritis; K capsule—pneumonia, neonatal meningitis; LPS endotoxin—septic shock."

**2. E. coli Strain Table (4 Rows)**
*Table Columns: STRAIN | TOXIN AND MECHANISM | PRESENTATION*

*   **Row 1: Enteroinvasive E coli**
    *   **TOXIN AND MECHANISM:** Microbe invades intestinal mucosa and causes necrosis and inflammation.
    *   **PRESENTATION:** EIEC is **I**nvasive; dysentery. Clinical manifestations similar to *Shigella*.

*   **Row 2: Enterotoxigenic E coli**
    *   **TOXIN AND MECHANISM:** Produces heat-labile and heat-stable entero**T**oxins. No inflammation or invasion.
    *   **PRESENTATION:** E**T**EC; **T**raveler’s diarrhea (watery).

*   **Row 3: Enteropathogenic E coli**
    *   **TOXIN AND MECHANISM:** No toxin produced. Adheres to apical surface, flattens villi, prevents absorption.
    *   **PRESENTATION:** Diarrhea, usually in children (think **EPEC** and **P**ediatrics).

*   **Row 4: Enterohemorrhagic E coli**
    *   **TOXIN AND MECHANISM:** O157:H7 is most common serotype in US. Often transmitted via undercooked meat, raw leafy vegetables.
        Shiga toxin causes **hemolytic-uremic syndrome**—triad of anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury due to microthrombi forming on damaged endothelium
        → mechanical hemolysis (with schistocytes on peripheral blood smear), platelet consumption
        → thrombocytopenia, and ↓ renal blood flow.
    *   **PRESENTATION:** Dysentery (toxin alone causes necrosis and inflammation).
        Does not ferment sorbitol (vs other *E coli*).
        EHEC associated with **h**emorrhage, **h**amburgers, **h**emolytic-uremic syndrome.

**3. Klebsiella Block**
*   **Header:** *Klebsiella* (Bold, Italic)
*   **Photograph Position:** To the left of the text block.
*   **Photograph Marker:** Letter **A** in the top-left corner of the image; asterisk (*) in the bottom-right corner.
*   **Full Text:** "Gram ⊖ rod; intestinal microbiota that causes lobar pneumonia; more common in patients with heavy alcohol use or with impaired host defenses. Very mucoid colonies **A** caused by abundant polysaccharide capsules. Dark red “currant jelly” sputum (blood/mucus). Also cause of healthcare-associated UTIs. Associated with evolution of multidrug resistance (MDR)."

**4. Campylobacter jejuni Block**
*   **Header:** *Campylobacter jejuni* (Bold, Italic)
*   **Photograph Position:** To the left of the text block.
*   **Photograph Markers:** Letter **A** in the top-left corner of the image; asterisk (*) in the bottom-right corner. (Note: The letter **A** also appears inline within the first sentence of the text).
*   **Full Text:** "Gram ⊖, comma or S shaped (with polar flagella) **A**, oxidase ⊕, grows at 42°C (“**Campylobacter likes the hot campfire**”).
Major cause of bloody diarrhea, especially in children. Fecal-oral transmission through person-to-person contact or via ingestion of undercooked contaminated poultry or meat, unpasteurized milk. Contact with infected animals (dogs, cats, pigs) is also a risk factor. Common antecedent to Guillain-Barré syndrome and reactive arthritis."

**5. Proteus mirabilis Block**
*   **Header:** *Proteus mirabilis* (Bold, Italic)
*   **Full Text:** "Gram ⊖, urease ⊕, facultative anaerobe, long flagellae with “swarming” motility.
Common cause of UTIs. Urease (virulence factor) hydrolyzes urea to carbon dioxide and ammonia
→ net increase in pH → promotes formation of struvite stones. Significant blockage of renal calyces results in branched stones called staghorn calculi."
